# Your subway clock 🚇

It's a clock, and it also shows the next **downtown trains at 81 St** in minutes:

**3  7  12  25** → trains in 3, 7, 12 and 25 minutes. **Orange** numbers are B trains, **blue**
are C trains (and late at night, when the B and C don't run, the A). **N** means it's arriving.
A **red dot** in the top-right corner means the MTA reports delays or other trouble on one of
these lines.
A gray **- -** means live times aren't available right now (no internet, or the MTA feed is
down). The clock keeps working. When no trains are running, the train screen just doesn't appear.

**Setting up**

1. Plug it in. After a few seconds it shows a setup screen.
2. On your phone, join the Wi-Fi network called **awtrixng-…** (no password).
3. A setup page opens by itself. If it doesn't, go to **192.168.4.1** in your browser.
4. Pick your home Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz), type its password, save. Done.

**New router, new password, or moving?**

Usually just plug it in: if it can't find its old network, it opens the **awtrixng-…** setup
network by itself after about 15 seconds. Then do steps 2–4 again.

To switch networks while the old one still works: unplug it, **hold the middle button**, plug it
back in and keep holding about a second until it says **SETUP**. Then steps 2–4.

Please don't use "factory reset" for Wi-Fi. It isn't needed and it erases your settings.

**Buttons:** left/right switch screens, a double-press of the middle button turns the display off
and on. The display dims itself in the dark, and turns off when the room is pitch black.

**A different train or station:** open the clock's address (shown at start-up) in a browser →
**Apps** → gear icon next to **Subway**. It works for any subway line: set the lines (e.g. `C`, or
up to three like `B,C,A`), the station ID and the direction (`N` or `S`). **Layout** there brings
back the line letters if you prefer them. The station list that came with the clock gives
the ID for every station and what N and S mean there.
